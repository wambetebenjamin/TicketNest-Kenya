Gengetone shows sell differently from corporate galas. The audience is young,
mobile-first and decides late. After three years of urban music events across
Nairobi, Kisumu and Mombasa, here is what the data says actually moves
tickets.

## Sell the fear of missing out, not the ticket

Gengetone fans buy in the final 72 hours before a show. Fighting this is
pointless. Instead, structure your tiers so late buying feels expensive:

1. **Early Bird (KES 1,000 to 1,500)** — release 4 to 6 weeks out, capped at
   20% of capacity. These are your superfans; treat them like it.
2. **Regular (KES 2,000 to 3,000)** — the workhorse tier.
3. **Final Release (KES 3,500+)** — open only in the last week, and say so in
   every caption.

When the Early Bird tier visibly sells out on the event page, Regular sales
spike within hours. Visible scarcity is your best salesperson.

## WhatsApp is your checkout's best friend

The single biggest conversion lift we see is the WhatsApp ticket delivery
button right after purchase. Buyers forward the ticket message to their group
chats, and every forward is a warm ad with a payment link attached. Make
sharing a feature, not an afterthought.

## STK push beats paybills

Sending the M-Pesa prompt directly to the buyer's phone (STK push) converts
significantly better than displaying a paybill number and hoping they
complete the transaction. Fewer taps, fewer abandoned carts.

## Squad pricing wins groups

A "Table of 6" or "Squad Bundle" tier at a 10 to 15% discount raises average
order value and turns one buyer into six attendees. Gengetone crowds move in
groups; price for it.

## Door discipline

Start the show on time even if the crowd is thin at 8 PM. The late wave
arrives after 9:30, and a strong first act playing to an empty room kills the
energy your TikTok clips need.

---

The formula is simple: visible scarcity, group pricing, mobile-first
checkout, and a door that moves. The rest is lineup.
